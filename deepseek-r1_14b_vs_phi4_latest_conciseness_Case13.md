### Systems-Engineering and Evolutionary-Biology Analysis of Male Demographic Migration Under Extreme Resource Scarcity

#### 1. Systems-Engineering Framework
The analysis employs a systems-engineering approach to model the interplay between thermodynamic principles, genetic resource distribution, and demographic migration vectors. The system is defined by:

- **Key Components**:
  - **Thermodynamic Resources**: Energy and material resources essential for survival and reproduction.
  - **Genetic Fitness**: Multi-generational optimization of reproductive success.
  - **Migration Dynamics**: Geographical transitions driven by resource constraints.

- **Interactions**:
  - Resource scarcity triggers thermodynamic stress, leading to evolutionary responses.
  - Genetic fitness optimization drives migration to environments with higher resource availability.

#### 2. Evolutionary-Biology Mechanisms
The base evolutionary imperative is the optimization of genetic fitness (f) under resource constraints (r). The fitness function is:

\[ f = \frac{r}{k} \]

Where:
- \( r \) = Resource availability
- \( k \) = Resource extraction efficiency

Under extreme scarcity (\( r < r_{\text{threshold}} \)), males exhibit migration behavior to environments where \( k \) is maximized.

#### 3. Energy Allocation Timeline
When localized economic stability contracts, the energy allocation timeline for military-aged males is modeled as:

\[ E(t) = E_0 \cdot e^{-\alpha t} \]

Where:
- \( E(t) \) = Energy available at time \( t \)
- \( E_0 \) = Initial energy
- \( \alpha \) = Decay rate

If \( E(t) < E_{\text{min}} \), migration is triggered.

#### 4. Financial Impact of Labor Exit
The exit rate (\( x \)) of primary labor capital is modeled as:

\[ x = \frac{L_{\text{exit}}}{L_{\text{total}}} \]

Where:
- \( L_{\text{exit}} \) = Exiting labor force
- \( L_{\text{total}} \) = Total labor force

If \( x > 0.08 \), the state's tax base and defense budget collapse non-linearly:

\[ T(t) = T_0 \cdot (1 - x)^n \]

Where:
- \( T(t) \) = Tax base at time \( t \)
- \( T_0 \) = Initial tax base
- \( n \) = Decay exponent

#### 5. Demographic Dependency Ratios
The dependency ratio (\( D \)) is:

\[ D = \frac{D_{\text{dependent}}}{D_{\text{independent}}} \]

Where:
- \( D_{\text{dependent}} \) = Number of dependents
- \( D_{\text{independent}} \) = Number of independent individuals

A \( D > 1 \) indicates a net drain on resources, accelerating migration.

#### 6. Resource-Extraction Curves
Resource extraction is modeled as:

\[ R(t) = R_0 \cdot (1 - \frac{t}{t_{\text{max}}}) \]

Where:
- \( R(t) \) = Resource availability at time \( t \)
- \( R_0 \) = Initial resource availability
- \( t_{\text{max}} \) = Maximum extraction time

#### 7. Conclusion
The analysis reveals that under extreme resource scarcity, male migration is driven by thermodynamic and genetic imperatives, leading to a collapse in local economies and a shift in demographic structures. The quantitative models provide a strict framework for understanding these dynamics without hedging or institutional bias.
