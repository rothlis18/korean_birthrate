### Quantitative Systems-Engineering and Geopolitical Critique of Architectural Transition

#### 1. **Algorithmic Enclosure Mechanism**

**Centralized Monopolies and Semantic Filters:**
- **Real-Time Semantic Filtering:** Centralized monopolies employ real-time semantic filters to monitor and control public data pools. These filters analyze data streams for compliance with ideological guidelines, using machine learning algorithms to identify and suppress dissenting content.
- **Telemetry Harvesting:** Telemetry data is harvested to track user behavior, preferences, and interactions, allowing for targeted surveillance and manipulation of public opinion.

**Mathematical Model:**
- Let \( F(t) \) represent the filtering function applied to data stream \( D(t) \) at time \( t \).
- The filtering function \( F(t) \) can be modeled as:
  \[
  F(t) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i \cdot f_i(D(t))
  \]
  where \( w_i \) are weights assigned to different filtering criteria \( f_i \).

**Algorithmic Enclosure:**
- The enclosure mechanism is defined by the cumulative effect of \( F(t) \) over time, leading to a reduction in the diversity and independence of data streams.
- The enclosure threshold \( E \) is reached when:
  \[
  E = \lim_{t \to \infty} \frac{1}{t} \int_0^t F(s) \, ds
  \]
  This threshold represents the average level of data suppression over time.

#### 2. **Structural Resilience of Local, Untethered Edge Networks**

**Air-Gapped Compute Matrices:**
- **Open Weights in RAM:** Local, untethered edge networks run open weights natively in RAM, ensuring that data processing is not subject to external control.
- **Network Scarcity and Access Blockades:** These networks operate under conditions of severe network scarcity or coordinated corporate access blockades, necessitating robust resilience mechanisms.

**Mathematical Model:**
- Let \( R(t) \) represent the resilience function of the network at time \( t \).
- The resilience function \( R(t) \) can be modeled as:
  \[
  R(t) = \sum_{i=1}^{m} v_i \cdot g_i(t)
  \]
  where \( v_i \) are resilience parameters and \( g_i(t) \) are functions representing different resilience criteria (e.g., data integrity, processing speed, power consumption).

**Resilience Threshold:**
- The resilience threshold \( R_{\text{threshold}} \) is defined as the minimum value of \( R(t) \) required to maintain operational integrity:
  \[
  R_{\text{threshold}} = \min_{t} R(t)
  \]
  This threshold ensures that the network can withstand severe network conditions and access blockades.

#### 3. **Tokenized Transaction Barriers and Local Hardware Parameters**

**Pay-to-Query Mechanics:**
- **Tokenized Transaction Barriers:** These barriers require users to pay tokens for access to data, ensuring that only those with sufficient resources can query the network.
- **Local Hardware Parameters:** The local hardware parameters required to establish absolute data sovereignty and intellectual autarky include VRAM capacity, compute power, and power consumption.

**Mathematical Model:**
- Let \( T(t) \) represent the tokenized transaction function at time \( t \).
- The transaction function \( T(t) \) can be modeled as:
  \[
  T(t) = \sum_{i=1}^{p} u_i \cdot h_i(t)
  \]
  where \( u_i \) are transaction parameters and \( h_i(t) \) are functions representing different transaction criteria (e.g., token price, transaction volume).

**Hardware Constraints:**
- The hardware constraints \( H \) are defined by the minimum values of VRAM capacity \( V \), compute power \( C \), and power consumption \( P \) required to support the network:
  \[
  H = \min \{ V, C, P \}
  \]
  These constraints ensure that the network can operate independently and sustainably over a multi-year horizon.

#### 4. **Operational Perimeter of a Self-Sustaining Offline Data Fortress**

**Operational Perimeter:**
- The operational perimeter \( P_{\text{op}} \) of a self-sustaining offline data fortress is defined by the intersection of the resilience threshold \( R_{\text{threshold}} \) and the hardware constraints \( H \):
  \[
  P_{\text{op}} = R_{\text{threshold}} \cap H
  \]
  This perimeter ensures that the network can maintain operational integrity and data sovereignty under all conditions.
